Self-supervised learning has recently demonstrated strong capabilities in speech representation learning by leveraging large amounts of unlabeled data. These models are able to learn contextual structure through reconstruction or prediction tasks, enabling them to generalize effectively across downstream applications such as speech recognition and speaker identification. However, different self-supervised models often exhibit complementary strengths, where some perform better on content-related tasks while others excel at speaker-related tasks. This suggests that combining multiple models may yield richer and more robust representations compared to relying on a single model.
